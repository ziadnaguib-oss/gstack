## Verdict: Referral Coordinator Inbox for GP-to-Specialist Handoffs

**Build**

Demand is strong and corroborated across independent sources — ideas.md's trade-press evidence and competitor-research.md's own competitor content agree on the same specific failure modes (lost visibility, repeated procedures, no status pings). Competitor-research.md is unusually clear that this is the least crowded of the three markets, with a genuine, named feature gap (surfacing prior-treatment history to the specialist) that neither of the two purpose-built competitors addresses. The two-sided "free for GP / paid for specialty hospital" monetization model is not speculative — it mirrors a live competitor's (VetLoop) actual pricing structure, and the scope (a status-tracking layer, not a PIMS replacement) is the most feasible of the three ideas to build with a small team. The main risks are execution risks (adoption dynamics, positioning against VetLoop, unverified price ceiling), not fundamental demand or market-structure problems — which is exactly the profile of a Build, not a Pivot or Kill.

### Top 3 risks

1. **Two-sided cold start: specialty hospitals pay for a dashboard that's only valuable if GPs actually use it.** If referring GP clinics keep defaulting to fax/email despite the tool being free, a specialty hospital ends up paying $200-400/mo for a mostly-empty inbox. Neither ideas.md nor competitor-research.md addresses how "free for GP" alone solves adoption — it only lowers the barrier, it doesn't guarantee behavior change.
   - De-risk: This week, interview 5 GP-side referral coordinators/office managers and 5 specialty-hospital intake staff with the same question: "If a free tool let you send/receive referral packets in two clicks with status pings, would you actually stop faxing for your next 10 referrals, or would this become another login you check once a month?" Bar: at least 4 of 5 GP-side respondents commit to a specific behavior change (naming a next referral they'd route through it), not just polite enthusiasm, before writing any GP-facing code.

2. **VetLoop already occupies near-identical positioning.** Competitor-research.md quotes VetLoop's own marketing as "the coordination layer veterinary medicine has been missing" — almost the same framing as this idea's own pitch, with the same free-for-GP model. If VetLoop already has signed specialty-hospital customers, a near-identical entrant has no wedge.
   - De-risk: Spend 2-3 hours this week directly reaching 3-5 specialty-hospital practice managers (LinkedIn/cold email) and ask if they've evaluated VetLoop or rVetLink and what stopped them from buying. If VetLoop already has real traction, don't clone its generic "status tracker" positioning — narrow the build to the one feature gap competitor-research.md explicitly flags as unaddressed by both incumbents: a structured "what's already been tried" summary surfaced to the specialist before the first visit. Ship and pitch that single differentiated feature first, not a full referral-inbox clone.

3. **The $200-400/mo specialty-hospital price is unverified.** Neither rVetLink nor VetLoop publishes pricing (both are demo-gated), so the proposed price band in ideas.md is an assumption, not a confirmed market-clearing number. If real comparable pricing turns out to be much lower, or IDEXX bundles rVetLink free into Cornerstone/ezyVet contracts, the standalone product can't sustain the assumed price.
   - De-risk: Build a one-page landing page this week for "the referral status dashboard for specialty hospitals" with the $200-400/mo price shown up front (deliberately undercutting rVetLink/VetLoop's demo-gated opacity, which competitor-research.md flags as a market-wide friction point). Drive it via direct outreach to 15-20 specialty-hospital practice managers. Bar: at least 3 reply asking to start a paid pilot at the stated price, without you first offering a discount — validates the price survives contact with the actual buyer, not just the (free-tier) referring GP.

### Next steps

1. Run the 10 GP/specialty interviews (risk 1) and the VetLoop/rVetLink competitive-displacement outreach (risk 2) in parallel this week — both are cheap and answer different questions.
2. Ship the priced landing page (risk 3) once interview framing is settled, so the pitch language matches what actually resonated in interviews.
3. If the interviews confirm GP-side behavior change and the landing page clears 3 paid-pilot replies at the stated price, scope an MVP around the single differentiated feature (prior-treatment-history summary) rather than the full two-sided platform, and pilot it with one specialty hospital + its 3-5 highest-referral-volume GP partners before broader build-out.
4. If either de-risk bar is missed (GP-side commitment weak, or no paid-pilot interest at the stated price), revisit as a Pivot: consider flipping to a specialty-hospital-only paid pilot using manual/email-based intake before investing in the GP-facing app, since the specialty side is the concentrated, revenue-bearing buyer.
